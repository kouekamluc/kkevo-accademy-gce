Solving Quadratic Equations by Factorization

Turn a quadratic into two simple linear equations and find both solutions.

Introduction

A quadratic like x² − 5x + 6 = 0 can have two solutions, so “get x on its own” doesn’t work. Factorization gives us a different strategy.

Definition
A quadratic equation can be written as
ax² + bx + c = 0
where a ≠ 0. Its solutions are called roots.

The zero product property

If two numbers multiply to give zero, at least one of them must be zero.

If A × B = 0, then
A = 0 or B = 0

Worked example 1

Solve  x² − 5x + 6 = 0
1
Identify b and c. b = −5, c = 6
2
Find the pair. Multiply to 6, add to −5:
−2 and −3 ✓
3
Factorize.
(x − 2)(x − 3) = 0
4
Split.
x − 2 = 0 or x − 3 = 0
5
Solve.
x = 2 or x = 3
Check: 4 − 10 + 6 = 0
Tip
If c is positive and b is negative, both numbers in the pair are negative.

Common mistakes

Not setting the equation to zero
x(x − 5) = 6 does not mean x = 6. Rearrange to x² − 5x − 6 = 0 first.
Dividing both sides by x
From x² = 3x you lose the root x = 0. Factorize instead.
