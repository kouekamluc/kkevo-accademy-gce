# Form 5 Geography: official source

The Form 5 Geography course follows the GCE Board's **revised 0550 Geography (Ordinary Level) syllabus**,
first examined in the June 2025 session. It is published by the Board at <https://camgceb.org/downloads/>:

- Syllabus: <https://camgceb.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/0550-GEOGRAPHY-SYLLABUS-REVIEW-2022.pdf>
- Circular of 18 November 2023 announcing the revision:
  <https://camgceb.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/COMMUNIQUE-ACCOMPANYING-REVISED-SYLLABUS-FOR-O55O-GEOGRAPHY-ORDINARY-LEVEL.pdf>

The outline in `apps/academics/gce_curriculum.py` summarises the syllabus (it is not verbatim) and the lessons are
KKEVO's own explanations of it. Check the Board's site for later revisions.

## Examination (from the syllabus)

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | 35% | 65% |
| Format | 50 multiple-choice questions, all answered | 7 essay and problem-solving questions, 4 answered |
| Time | 1 hour 30 minutes | 2 hours 30 minutes |
| Marks | 50 | 90 |

- Paper 1 questions: map skills 5, physical geography 23, human geography 22. No multiple-choice question is set on
  case studies except on Cameroon.
- Paper 2 sections: A Map reading and interpretation (1 question, compulsory), B Physical geography (2, answer 1),
  C Human geography with examples from LICs, NICs and AICs (2, answer 1), D Geography of Cameroon (2, answer 1).
- The map reading question is compulsory and carries 25% of the total marks for the whole examination.
- Weighting of assessment objectives: knowledge 35%, comprehension 40%, application 20%, analysis 5%.

## Scope notes from the Board's list of changes

Removed from O Level: gradient calculation and cross-sections (gradient is described from contour lines),
glaciation and limestone (karst) landscapes, soil-forming processes and biomes other than tropical rainforest,
savanna and desert, isostasy, Boserup's model, the "brief history of Cameroon" lesson. Drainage patterns are limited to
dendritic, trellised and radial. Folding, faulting, mountains, plains and plateaus are treated as processes at plate
margins. Case studies are not required for agriculture and resources (examples are enough).
Added: climate-smart agriculture, food loss and food waste, water scarcity and waste management as environmental hazards.
