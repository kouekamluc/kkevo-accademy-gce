# Form 5 Logic: official source

The Form 5 Logic course follows the GCE Board's **revised 0590 Logic (Ordinary Level) syllabus**, first examined in the
June 2025 session (document dated March 2024), published at <https://camgceb.org/downloads/>:

- Syllabus: <https://camgceb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/0590-LOGIC-Syllabus-Review.pdf>

The outline in `apps/academics/gce_curriculum.py` follows the syllabus's nine modules and numbered topics (it is not
verbatim); the lessons are KKEVO's own explanations. The Board lists topics only, without detailed objectives, so the
lessons stay within what each topic names. Check the Board's site for later revisions.

## Aims (summary)

Basic concepts and procedures of logic and correct use of language; critical thinking and elementary symbolic logic;
organising coherent explanations and arguments; identifying and detecting fallacious reasoning. Logic is a prerequisite
for A Level Philosophy.

## Examination (from the syllabus)

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | 40% | 60% |
| Format | 50 multiple-choice questions, all answered, 1 mark each | 6 questions: Section A structural (3 questions, all answered), Section B problem-solving (3 questions, any 2 answered); 20 marks each |
| Time | 1 hour 30 minutes | 2 hours 30 minutes |

Assessment objectives: knowledge 40%, understanding 30%, application 20%, higher-level abilities 10%.
Questions apply basic principles to situations such as ethics, environment, economy, religion, culture, management and
jurisprudence.

## Symbols the syllabus selects

Negation (~), conjunction (.), disjunction/alternation (v), material implication (horseshoe, written ⊃),
biconditional/equivalence (written ≡).

## References named by the Board

Copi and Cohen, Introduction to Logic (2016); Layman, The Power of Logic (2005); Moore and Parker, Critical Thinking
(2000); Hurley, A Concise Introduction to Logic (11th ed., 2023); Luce, Logic (1958); Hausman, Boardman and Kahane,
Logic and Philosophy (2021).
