Solving Quadratic Equations by Factorization

Learn how to turn a quadratic equation into two simple linear equations, and find both of its solutions.

18 min read 4 min video 5 practice questions Requires: Factorizing Quadratics

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  • Recognise a quadratic equation in standard form
  • Explain why the zero product property lets us split an equation
  • Solve quadratics where a = 1 and where a ≠ 1
  • Check your solutions by substitution

Introduction

In the last lesson you met equations like x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Unlike a linear equation, a quadratic can have two solutions, so the usual “get x on its own” approach doesn’t work.

Factorization gives us a different strategy: rewrite the left-hand side as a product of two brackets, then use one simple fact about multiplying by zero.

Definition
A quadratic equation is any equation that can be written in the form
ax² + bx + c = 0,   where a ≠ 0.
The values of x that make it true are called its roots or solutions.

The key idea: the zero product property

If two numbers multiply to give zero, at least one of them must be zero. There is no other way to get zero from a multiplication.

If  A × B = 0,  then  A = 0  or  B = 0

So if we can write a quadratic as (x − p)(x − q) = 0, its roots are simply x = p and x = q.

Worked example 2: when a ≠ 1

When the x² term has a coefficient, we look for two numbers that multiply to a × c instead, then split the middle term.

Solve  2x² + 7x + 3 = 0You are here
1
Multiply a by c
ac = 2 × 3 = 6
2
Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 7
They are 6 and 1. Use them to split 7x into 6x + x.
3
Factorize in pairs
2x² + 6x + x + 3 = 0
2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = 0
(2x + 1)(x + 3) = 0
4
Solve each bracket
x = −½   or   x = −3

Common mistakes

Not setting the equation to zero first
x(x − 5) = 6 does not mean x = 6 or x − 5 = 6. Move everything to one side first: x² − 5x − 6 = 0.
Dividing both sides by x
From x² = 3x, dividing by x gives only x = 3 and loses x = 0. Factorize instead: x(x − 3) = 0.
Getting the sign of the root wrong
The bracket (x + 3) gives the root x = −3, not +3.

Summary

  1. Rearrange so the equation equals zero.
  2. Factorize the quadratic into two brackets (use ac when a ≠ 1).
  3. Set each bracket equal to zero and solve.
  4. Check both answers by substituting them back.
